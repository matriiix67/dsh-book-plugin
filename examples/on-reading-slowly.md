# On Reading Slowly

A short essay for trying the bookshelf's dictionary, translation, notes and citations.

## Why slow down

Most of us skim. We scan headlines, skip paragraphs, and abandon chapters halfway through. Slow reading asks for the opposite: to stay with a sentence until it opens up, and to notice how an author builds an argument rather than racing to the conclusion.

## A pencil and a question

Keep a pencil nearby. Underline the passages that surprise you, and write one question in the margin before you turn the page. The question does not need an answer yet; it simply keeps your attention anchored to the text.

## Words worth looking up

When an unfamiliar word appears, resist the urge to guess. Looking it up takes a few seconds, and the meaning often reshapes the whole paragraph. Over weeks, these small detours quietly enlarge your vocabulary.

## Returning to your notes

A week later, revisit what you highlighted. Some notes will feel obvious; others will still spark curiosity. Those are the ideas worth carrying into your own work and conversations.
